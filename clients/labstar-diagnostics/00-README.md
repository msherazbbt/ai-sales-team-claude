## Important Notes on Data & Compliance

1. **Phase 1 prospect data**: This plan includes a *seed sample* of real, publicly-listed practices and facilities found via web research (sources cited inline), organized into the requested category databases. **Administrator names, office manager names, decision makers, in-house lab relationships, and exact monthly volumes are NOT publicly available** and must be gathered through the discovery process described in the Sales Strategy (Phase 3) — this is, by design, the job of the first few outreach touches. Treat the seed data as a **starting call list**, not a verified database. Section 1 includes a full SOP and schema for your team (or a VA/data service) to build out the complete database of "every potential customer" — realistically 800–1,500+ facilities across the full 50-mile radius.
2. **Opportunity Scores** in Phase 1/2 are *estimated* based on practice type, independence, size signals, and proximity — refine them after first contact using the CRM fields in Phase 8.
3. **Compliance**: Any Model B (direct-to-consumer/telehealth) program must be designed with CLIA, state lab-licensing, anti-kickback/Stark law, and corporate practice of medicine rules in mind. Phase 6 lays out the guardrails — **have healthcare counsel review the final telehealth agreement before launch.**
4. **Routes/mileage** in Phase 5 are estimated from road geography around Hatfield, PA and should be validated with an actual courier driving the route before committing to a fixed schedule/cost.

## Executive Summary

Labstar's strategic advantage over Quest/Labcorp/regional reference labs is **speed, locality, and relationship**: same-day or 1-hour turnaround on CLIA-waived urinalysis/UA-hCG performed *on-site at Labstar*, versus next-day-or-later results from a hub lab 1–3 hours away. The market within 50 miles of Hatfield is dense — Montgomery, Bucks, and parts of Philadelphia/Chester/Delaware counties contain hundreds of independent primary care, urgent care, pediatric, and post-acute facilities that are either sending urine specimens to Quest/Labcorp (slow, impersonal, contracts that lock out flexibility) or running in-house dipstick testing with no QC oversight, no microscopy backup, and CLIA compliance risk.

**The play:**
1. Build a prioritized prospect database (Phase 1–2), starting with the ~15-mile "home turf" ring around Hatfield (Lansdale, North Wales, Montgomeryville, Souderton, Telford, Harleysville, Colmar, Chalfont, Hatboro, Horsham, Ambler).
2. Run a high-touch outbound motion (Phase 3) targeting independent practices and post-acute facilities — the segments most likely to switch quickly and most volume-dense for UA/hCG.
3. Win on same-day courier pickup + same-day results + a real phone number that's answered by a real person (Phase 4–5).
4. Layer in a compliant Model B telehealth/DTC channel for incremental volume (Phase 6).
5. Track everything in a lightweight CRM (Phase 8), execute a disciplined 90-day cold-start plan (Phase 9), and compound with referral/partnership loops to become the default local lab (Phase 10).
