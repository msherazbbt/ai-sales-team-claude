# PHASE 10 — Domination Plan: Becoming the Preferred Local Lab

## 10.1 Competitive Advantages to Build & Reinforce

| Advantage | How to Build It |
|---|---|
| **Speed (same-day TAT)** | Operationally validate and then *publish* turnaround data (e.g., "94% of specimens resulted within 90 minutes of pickup, March 2026"). Make this a recurring scorecard shared with accounts quarterly. |
| **Hyper-local presence** | Sponsor/attend local events (chamber of commerce, healthcare provider mixers in Lansdale/North Penn area); Labstar staff become recognizable faces at provider offices. |
| **Named relationships** | Every account has a named lab liaison with direct cell/text — track liaison response time as an internal KPI (target <30 min during business hours). |
| **No-lock-in flexibility** | Maintain "no exclusivity required" as a standing policy — make it part of every pitch and contract. This lowers switching friction *into* Labstar and reduces switching *away* once the relationship is valued. |
| **Expanding test menu** | As CLIA-waived menu expands (Phase 0 of original brief mentions "future expansion"), proactively notify existing accounts — each new test is a reason to re-engage every account in the CRM. |

## 10.2 Referral Strategies

- **Ask every Won account for 2 referrals within 30 days of onboarding** (built into onboarding checklist) — independent practices often share office managers' associations, physician referral networks, or are part of IPAs (Independent Practice Associations).
- **IPA/PHO relationships:** Identify if Tier A independent practices belong to a local Independent Practice Association or Physician-Hospital Organization (common in Montgomery/Bucks County) — a single presentation to an IPA's member meeting can open doors to dozens of practices at once.
- **Referral incentive (compliance-checked):** Consider a modest, non-monetary referral incentive for B2B accounts (e.g., free supply restock, priority STAT pickup) — **avoid cash/value-based incentives tied to referral of patients or testing volume**, which can raise anti-kickback concerns; incentives should be framed around *introductions to other businesses*, not patient referrals.

## 10.3 Partnership Strategies

| Partner Type | Partnership Angle |
|---|---|
| **Independent pharmacies** | Pharmacies often field questions about UTI symptoms/pregnancy testing — partner for patient referrals to a nearby provider + Labstar testing pathway (compliant referral pathway, not direct lab-to-consumer without provider order for Model A). |
| **EMS / non-emergency medical transport companies** | Potential courier-route synergy and AL/SNF relationship overlap. |
| **Local hospitals' discharge planning departments** | For post-discharge home health patients needing follow-up UA — position Labstar as the fast local option for home health agencies (Phase 1.2.F) coordinating post-discharge care. |
| **Employers (mid-size local businesses)** | Pre-employment and random workplace urinalysis drug screening — target HR departments of manufacturing/logistics employers in the Hatfield/Lansdale industrial corridor (e.g., businesses near County Line Rd / Route 309 corridor). |

## 10.4 Community Outreach

- **Sponsor a local 5K, school health fair, or senior center health screening day** — branding + goodwill, and a natural lead-in to AL/community health conversations.
- **"Lunch and learn" sessions** at independent practices — bring lunch, do a 15-minute walkthrough of the CLINITEK Status+ process, QC documentation, and how same-day reporting works. Low-pressure, educational framing builds trust faster than a sales pitch.
- **Local business associations** (North Penn Chamber of Commerce, Indian Valley Chamber of Commerce, Greater Norristown Chamber) — membership and networking events build the "local business supporting local businesses" narrative that's part of the core value prop (Phase 4).

## 10.5 Physician Education Programs

- **CLIA waiver compliance briefings** for practices doing in-house testing — position Labstar as a resource/partner for QC, proficiency testing support, and confirmatory testing, not just a competitor to in-house testing (ties to the "We do testing in-house" objection handling, Phase 3.10).
- **UTI management update sessions** — brief, evidence-based content on same-day UA's impact on antibiotic stewardship (avoiding empiric prescribing) — appeals to physicians' clinical interests and naturally highlights Labstar's turnaround advantage.
- **Newsletter/flyer series** (started Week 11 of the 90-day plan) — quarterly 1-page updates: turnaround stats, new test menu additions, local healthcare news, profile of a local provider partner.

## 10.6 Assisted-Living & SNF Partnerships

- **STAT UTI rule-out protocol partnerships**: Even AL/SNF facilities with existing national-lab contracts have gaps — propose a "STAT supplemental" agreement specifically for after-hours/urgent UA needs that their primary lab's courier schedule doesn't cover same-day. This is the most realistic wedge into Tier B/C SNF accounts (Phase 1.2.E).
- **In-service training for nursing staff**: Offer brief on-site trainings on proper specimen collection/labeling — reduces rejected specimens, builds relationships with charge nurses (often the actual day-to-day decision-influencers even when administration signs contracts).
- **Medical director relationships**: AL/SNF medical directors often oversee multiple facilities — winning one relationship can open doors to their full portfolio.

## 10.7 Employer Partnerships

- Target mid-size employers (50–500 employees) in the Hatfield/Lansdale/Montgomeryville industrial and logistics corridor for:
  - Pre-employment drug screening (UDS component within CLIA-waived scope)
  - DOT-required testing referrals (partner with a DOT-certified collector/MRO if Labstar doesn't hold this directly)
  - Wellness-day urinalysis screening events (e.g., annual health fairs)
- Position via local HR/SHRM chapter networking and direct outreach to HR departments (similar cadence to Phase 3, adapted messaging: "fast, local pre-employment screening turnaround so new hires can start sooner").

## 10.8 Expansion Roadmap

| Timeframe | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **Months 1–3** | Execute 90-day plan (Phase 9): 30–40 accounts, 500–1,000 specimens/month, 5-route network validated |
| **Months 4–6** | Cross break-even (~1,800–1,900 specimens/month, Phase 7); launch Model B telehealth pilot (Phase 6) with urine pregnancy test as lead product |
| **Months 6–9** | Expand CLIA-waived test menu (e.g., rapid strep, flu/COVID antigen, glucose, A1c, lipid panel point-of-care — confirm with lab director and update CLIA certificate scope); re-engage full account list with "new test menu" outreach (Phase 10.1) |
| **Months 9–12** | Activate Routes D/E daily; expand geographic research to full 50-mile ring (towns beyond initial 25-mile research, including Philadelphia Northeast, Reading/Pottstown corridor, Lehigh Valley edge, NJ border towns) |
| **Year 2** | Evaluate satellite specimen drop-off/draw site in a secondary cluster (e.g., Doylestown or Quakertown) to extend same-day coverage further into Bucks County; expand Model B to additional states pending compliance review (Phase 6.9) |
| **Year 2+** | Position Labstar as the **default local reference lab for independent practices in the North Penn/Indian Valley/Bucks County corridor** — measured by market share of independent (non-health-system) practices within 15 miles using Labstar for routine UA/hCG |

## 10.9 KPIs to Track Long-Term

| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| Active accounts | 40 (Day 90) → 80 (Month 6) → 150+ (Year 1) |
| Monthly specimen volume | 500–1,000 (Day 90) → 1,800+ (Month 6, break-even) → 3,000+ (Year 1) |
| Average TAT (pickup to result) | <2 hours, 90%+ of specimens |
| Account churn rate | <5% monthly |
| Referrals generated per quarter | 2 per new account within first 90 days |
| Net Promoter Score (account satisfaction, quarterly survey) | 50+ |
| % of Tier A prospects (50-mile radius) converted | 50% by end of Year 1, 70%+ by end of Year 2 |
